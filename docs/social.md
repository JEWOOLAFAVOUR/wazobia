# Social & Community Life

The city should feel like a real place where people live, not just an economic spreadsheet.

Players should be able to work, make money, build businesses and property, but also:

- Talk to people
- Make friends
- Attend religious services
- Join clubs
- Go to parties
- Attend concerts
- Watch football
- Visit restaurants
- Go to parks
- Attend weddings
- Meet romantic partners
- Join communities
- Create their own organizations
- Host events
- Build reputations

The economic simulation gives the city its structure.

The social simulation gives it its **soul**.

---

# 1. Player Chat

Players should be able to communicate naturally.

### Global chat

A city-wide channel.

```text
[Lagos]

Favour:
Anybody going Yaba → VI?

Tunde:
I dey go around 5.

Ada:
Anyone selling a 2-bedroom apartment in Yaba?

Kunle:
My restaurant just opened 😂
```

### Local chat

Only people near the player can see it.

```text
[Yaba]

"Who knows where I can get good food?"

"Traffic for Herbert Macaulay is crazy."

"Anybody going to the meetup tonight?"
```

### Business chat

Companies can have private channels.

### Group chat

Friends and organizations can create groups.

---

# 2. Proximity-Based Social Interaction

The closer players are physically located in the game, the more they should be able to interact.

A player walking around Yaba might see:

```text
👤 12 players nearby
```

They can:

- Say hello
- Start a conversation
- Trade
- Invite someone somewhere
- Follow someone
- Add them as a friend
- Offer a job
- Start a business conversation

This makes physical location meaningful.

# 4. Churches

Churches should exist as normal parts of the city.

Players can:

- Attend services
- Join a congregation
- Meet people
- Volunteer
- Donate
- Attend events
- Join small groups
- Participate in community activities

Churches can also have:

```text
Members
Leaders
Buildings
Events
Donations
Community projects
```

Players could eventually create or manage community organizations where appropriate.

The game should represent different denominations and styles rather than treating "church" as one generic place.

---

# 5. Mosques

Mosques should have equivalent social functionality.

Players can:

- Attend prayers
- Join communities
- Meet people
- Attend religious/community events
- Volunteer
- Donate
- Participate in charitable activities

Again, the goal is not to turn religion into a scoring mechanic.

It should simply be part of everyday city life.

# 10. Player-Created Events

Players should eventually be able to create events.

Example:

> **Lagos Developer Meetup**

```text
Location: Yaba
Date: Saturday
Capacity: 200
Ticket: ₦5,000
Organizer: Favour
```

The organizer needs to:

- Find a venue
- Set a price
- Advertise
- Hire staff
- Manage capacity
- Sell tickets

Other players can:

- Attend
- Sell food
- Provide transportation
- Provide security
- Perform
- Sponsor the event

One event can therefore create an entire temporary economy.

---

# 11. Weddings

Major life events should exist.

Two players can eventually:

- Become friends
- Start dating
- Become partners
- Get married
- Move into a home together
- Combine finances
- Have social events

A wedding could involve:

```text
Venue
Food
Clothing
Photography
Music
Guests
Transportation
Decorations
```

Which means dozens of players and businesses can participate.

The game should allow relationships without forcing them into a single "romance" progression.

# 12. Friendships

Friendship should develop through repeated interactions.

Instead of:

> Click "Become Friends"

use:

```text
Meet
 ↓
Talk
 ↓
Interact again
 ↓
Shared activity
 ↓
Trust
 ↓
Friendship
```

Friendship can unlock:

- Group chats
- Invitations
- Business opportunities
- Social events
- Shared activities
- Help
- Loans
- Housing arrangements

Relationships should emerge from gameplay.

---

# 13. Reputation

Players should develop social reputations.

Not simply:

> Reputation = 87

Instead, the city gradually learns who they are.

Someone might become known as:

> "That guy who always pays his workers."

Another:

> "She's a really good developer."

Another:

> "Don't do business with him."

Reputation can affect:

- Hiring
- Loans
- Business partnerships
- Customers
- Housing
- Invitations
- Social relationships

Actions should matter.

The same systems should affect the player's personal life.

Example:

```text
You work constantly.
        ↓

        ↓
You have less free time.
        ↓

        ↓

```

```text

        ↓

        ↓
```

```text

Relationships
```

# 18. Community Projects

Players should eventually be able to pool resources.

Example:

A neighborhood has poor roads.

Residents create:

> **Yaba Community Association**

They contribute money.

```text
1,000 residents
×
₦5,000
=
₦5,000,000
```

They use it to fund a community project.

Other projects:

- Security
- Clean-up
- Street lighting
- Events
- Sports facilities
- Community centers

This makes players feel like they are actually part of a neighborhood.

# 20. The "Where Everyone Is" Effect

The game should occasionally create obvious social hotspots.

For example:

> **🔥 Something is happening in Yaba tonight**

Hundreds of players begin moving there.

A venue becomes crowded.

Nearby restaurants become busy.

Transport prices increase.

People start chatting.

Someone starts streaming the event.

Businesses notice the opportunity.

This creates organic social events.

# 23. Stories Should Emerge

The game should not constantly tell the player what story to follow.

The systems should create stories.

Example:

> Two friends start a restaurant.

↓

It becomes successful.

↓

One wants to expand.

↓

The other wants to stay small.

↓

They split.

↓

One opens another restaurant.

↓

They become competitors.

↓

One starts a delivery company.

↓

The other partners with a rival.

Suddenly two players have a multi-month story that the game never explicitly scripted.

That is the ultimate goal.

---

# 24. The Feeling We Want

The player should eventually say:

> "This is my neighborhood."

Then:

> "That's my business."

Then:

> "Those are my friends."

Then:

> "I helped build this place."

And eventually:

> "I've been playing on this server for two years."

That is the emotional payoff of a persistent world.

The player isn't just progressing through levels.

**They are leaving a mark on a shared city.**

---

# 25. Social Engagement Principles

The game should encourage engagement through:

### Curiosity

"What happened while I was gone?"

### Agency

"I decided to do this."

### Mastery

"I understand the economy now."

### Belonging

"These are my people."

### Ownership

"That's my company."

### Identity

"That's what I'm known for."

### Discovery

"I didn't know this existed."

### Story

"I can't believe that happened."

### Legacy

"I built that."

These are more valuable than simply forcing the player to log in every day.

---

# 26. Ultimate Vision

The final experience should feel like:

> **A persistent online Lagos where people live, work, socialize, build businesses, form communities, create relationships and collectively shape the city.**

Someone can spend an evening doing nothing economically productive.

They can simply:

> Meet friends → eat → attend a concert → talk → go home.

Another player can spend that same evening:

> Trading → buying property → negotiating a business deal.

Another:

> Building an apartment complex.

Another:

> Running a nightclub.

Another:

> Organizing a church/community event.

Another:

> Playing football.

All of them are participating in the **same world**.

That is the game.
